## 1. The finding this plan answers

Both application templates — the model template
(`PropertyKindCalculus.Rubrics.modelTemplate`, argued in the blueprint chapter *Applying the
calculus to a domain*) and the deployment template (`deploymentTemplate`, *Deploying a
domain model*) — are discharged by **sites**: a document names
one declaration and one section per rubric, and `Conformance.status` reports the rubric's
`dischargedWord` once each channel its evidence kind requires has a site. A site is one
thing. It is never a claim about the rest.

Read against soil-moisture-model's technical reference, that produced a concrete gap
(2026-09-07):

* M6 — *kinds of dimension one are individuated by their examination principle* — is
  scored `generated` on the strength of two annotated theorems in
  `algorithm/dielectric/examination/coherence.lean`. A probe over the same module set the
  conformance report scores found **100 `KindOfProperty` constants under `SoilMoisture`,
  75 with `examPrinciple = none`** — among them the four dimension-one kinds the model
  proves are PKC's own motivating example (volumetric and gravimetric water content,
  relative permittivity, reflectivity), whose distinctness rests on `by decide` over their
  `id` strings. A third principle-separated theorem in the same file
  (`transitionMoisture_ne_relPerm`) carries no annotation at all.
* The mechanism cannot see any of this. `Conformance.status` tests whether the site list
  is non-empty; two theorems and two hundred read identically.

The general form: **a rubric discharged by exemplars is only as complete as the author's
memory.** The rest of this document is about which rubrics can be discharged by anything
else, and how.

## 2. The framing (fixed in the catalogue; argued in the blueprint)

Each rubric now carries a `closure` (`PropertyKindCalculus.Rubrics.Closure`), orthogonal to
its evidence kind:

| closure | meaning |
|---|---|
| `prose` | the population is what the document *says*; no census is possible, by construction |
| `pending population` | the population can be enumerated from the environment; nothing walks it yet |
| `record mechanism` | a generated view or a `#guard_msgs`-pinned reading — cannot omit a member; a changed answer is a visible diff |
| `gate mechanism` | an audit that fails the build on a member that does not satisfy the rubric |

A census needs three things, and each either exists or does not: **a population that is a
type** (declaring a member enrolls it — `#kind_contracts` is the model); **a decidable
per-member predicate**; **declarable exceptions** (`@[kindCounterexample]`, the declared
clusters of `#kind_scc_clean`). The full argument is the chapter section *What the machine
can hold, and what it cannot*; this file does not repeat it.

Profile at baseline, generated by `rubric_closure_count` (not typed here — re-run it):
model template 26 = 5 prose + 10 held (4 gated) + 11 pending; deployment template
17 = 7 prose + 5 held (2 gated) + 5 pending.

## 3. The plan

The plan covers both templates. The eleven pending model rubrics and the five pending
deployment rubrics are not one kind of work.

| bucket | rubrics | what the work actually is |
|---|---|---|
| population exists — write the sweep | M6, M12, M15, M21 (domain half) — **done** | a command of the `#kind_*` shape over a type already in the environment |
| population must be *declared* first | M17, M20, M22, M21 (ambiguity half), M18, M19, M26 (and D6, D7, D8, D13) | choose the declaration form — a bridge as data, a well-posedness witness and its domain, a diagnostic port, a measurement, an adequacy claim, an uncertainty record — then the sweep is trivial |
| a proxy must be chosen | M7 | "relied on" has no mechanical meaning; someone decides the proxy and writes down that it is one |
| population lives downstream | D3 | the declared payload faces are a deployment repository's types (soil-moisture-workflows, sm-smap-nisar-lean), so the sweep is written there against PKC's receipt API, not here |

The deployment template's held rows are D4 (`#kind_discharges_decide`, a PKC command,
receipted), D5/D10/D16 (generators), and D12 — whose gate is the acceptance run itself, an
executable outside PKC. D12 cannot receipt through this extension and stays a declaration
site; a deployment document's matrix should say so rather than read it as walked.

And one item that is not a sweep at all, and comes first:

### Step 1 — make a green mean the census ran (done 2026-09-07)

Audits leave **receipts**. `PropertyKindCalculus.AuditReceipt` is an environment
extension; each instrumented command records `{ audit, scope, module }` when it completes
(for a `_clean` gate: when it *passes* — it throws otherwise, so a receipt is a pass).
`Conformance.status` then requires, for a rubric whose closure names audits, a receipt for
each named audit whose scope covers the document's `declScope`. A document that annotates
two theorems for a gated rubric and never runs the gate reads `partial`, not green.

Instrumented: `#kind_boundary_clean`, `#kind_mint_ratchet` (M9);
`#kind_dimensional_coverage`, `#kind_dimensional_clean`, `#kind_scc`, `#kind_scc_clean`
(M10); `#kind_contracts`, `#kind_contracts_decide` (M11 — the decide form also leaves the
report form's receipt, since it is the same sweep with kernel receipts); `#kind_relations`
(M13, M14); `#kind_discharges_decide` (D4); `#kind_examination_coverage`,
`#kind_examination_clean` (M6, step 2).

What it does not verify: that the named module contains the *right* audit over the *right*
namespaces beyond a prefix test, or that a pinned reading's pin is current. The receipt
says the command ran in this build over that scope. That is the fact a matrix can honestly
report, and it is a strictly stronger fact than "a site exists".

Scoping rule for the receipt check: an audit ran over namespaces `nss`; the document's
`declScope` is `ns`. The receipt covers the document iff some element of `nss` is a prefix
of `ns` or equal to it. A document with no `declScope` accepts any receipt for the audit,
which is right only for a document whose environment contains no other — the same caveat
`declScope` already carries.

### Step 2 — the M6 sweep (done 2026-09-07; SMM run done the same day, and it changed the sweep)

`#kind_examination_coverage ns …` (record) and `#kind_examination_clean ns …` (gate), in
`PropertyKindCalculus.ExaminationCoverage` (the `Dimension` library, beside
`DimensionalCoverage`, whose kind-resolution it reuses).

Population: every `DimensionedKind` declaration in scope whose dimension is one — decided
per generator by kernel reduction of the ℚ exponents, exactly as the dimensional-coverage
walk decides coherence. Not `KindOfProperty` constants: "dimension one" is a fact about the
`DimensionedKind`, the ISO 80000 catalogue declares most of its kinds inline inside one,
and a kind with no dimension declared is M10's finding, not M6's.

Predicate: the kind carries an examination principle (`examPrinciple ≠ none`).

Exception: `@[kindPrincipleFree "reason"]` on the `DimensionedKind` declaration. The
sweep lists such kinds as `⊘ exempted` with the reason; the gate does not fire on them.
This is the idiom the fresnel geometry kinds in soil-moisture-model need — today their
exemption is a docstring no audit can read.

Verdicts per row: `[individuated]` (has a principle), `⚠ UNINDIVIDUATED` (dimension one,
no principle, no mark), `⊘ exempted`, and — added after the soil-moisture-model run —
`⚠ UNDIMENSIONED`: a bare `KindOfProperty` constant in scope that no `DimensionedKind`
anywhere in the environment wraps. The report's last line counts each. The gate throws
while any row is `UNINDIVIDUATED` or `UNDIMENSIONED`, with the same no-message discipline
as the other `_clean` commands, and its error names the fixes and refuses the re-pin.

**What the SMM run found, and why the fourth verdict exists.** The first run read
`no dimension-one kinds in the given namespaces` over `SoilMoisture` — and the gate would
have passed. soil-moisture-model declares its kinds as bare `KindOfProperty` constants and
never a `DimensionedKind`, so the population as first defined was empty for exactly the
vocabulary M6 was written about, and a gate over an empty population is the vacuous
universal the test suite's charter warns against. The census now reports such kinds and the
gate fires on them: over `SoilMoisture` it reads **0 dimension-one kind(s); 96 kind(s)
UNDIMENSIONED**. The honest reading is that M6 cannot be decided for that model until it
declares dimensions (M10's obligation), and the matrix should say `partial` until then
rather than green. Row moved to `gate` on that basis.

### Step 3 — the remaining sweeps (done 2026-09-07 for M12, M15, M21's domain half)

Before writing, each of the six was explored against the types that exist. Three had a
population that is a type *and* a decidable predicate; three did not, and one was half of
each. The three are built; the rest are re-bucketed below with the reason and the form
they need.

`PropertyKindCalculus.ContractCoverage` (core, beside `KindIncidence`): three censuses over
declared boundaries, each a record command, a `_clean` gate, receipts at the success points,
and a probe (`Tests.Core.ContractCoverage`) that pins every verdict class of every census,
the three gate refusals, three silent gates over a clean sub-namespace, and the two
attributes' own refusals.

* **M12 — `#kind_relation_coverage` / `#kind_relation_clean`.** Population:
  `contractSweepSubjects` (every `Provenance.Contract` in scope). Predicate: some
  `Provenance.Relation` names the contract on either side — relations walked *unscoped*,
  `@[kindCounterexample]` relations excluded, the join lifted out of `Index.Contracts` so the
  record table and the gate share it. Exception: `@[kindRelationFree "reason"]` on the
  contract. Validity of the edge stays `#kind_relations`' question (M13/M14).
* **M15 — `#kind_mereology_coverage` / `#kind_mereology_clean`.** Population: every produced
  port (`output` or `conditional`) of every contract in scope. Predicate: an `aggregations`
  entry for the port. No exception mark — the `AggregationClass` vocabulary is total over
  the honest negatives (`.intensive`, `.wholeProper`, `.countKeyed`, …), so "declares
  nothing" is exactly the finding.
* **M21, the domain half — `#kind_inversion_coverage` / `#kind_inversion_clean`.**
  Population: every contract in scope that is the *left* side of an `inverts` relation.
  Predicate: a `conditional` port (its decider reported where named). Exception:
  `@[kindInversionTotal "reason"]`. The ambiguity half ("surfaced rather than resolved to the
  first root") has no declaration form and is not walked; the row's closure says so.

**Re-bucketed to step 4, with the code facts that decided it:**

* **M17.** The population exists — the `Carrier`/`NumCarrier` instance vocabulary, with
  `LawfulCarrier` as the line between specification and executable — but the predicate does
  not: a bridge is a `CarrierRefinement` instance only in PKC's own examples, while every
  bridge the worked instance names (`.magnitude` parity, tape parity, the rounded constant
  table) is a *theorem*, and `IEEE32Exec` is deliberately unbridged with the reason in a
  docstring. A sweep today would report every real bridge as missing.
* **M20.** The `inverts` edges are enumerable, but `Provenance.Relation` has no field for an
  existence-and-uniqueness witness or the domain it holds on, and `checkRelation` requires
  the `witness` to conclude with a round-trip `Eq` — so an `∃!` theorem such as the worked
  instance's `retrieval_well_posed` is structurally inadmissible where the edge lives.
* **M22.** Produced ports are enumerable, but nothing marks a port or a kind as a quality or
  conditioning output; the worked instance declares those as `IngestContract` slots and as
  kinds, not as ports of any `Provenance.Contract`. The population is not yet a type.
* **M21's ambiguity half.** No constructor, field or role anywhere expresses "more than one
  root, and said so"; `retrieval_ambiguous_of_collision` exists as a theorem hanging off no
  declaration.

### Step 4 — the declaration designs

M17, M20, M22 and M21's ambiguity half, from step 3; M18, M19, M26 in the model template;
D6, D7, D8, D13 in the deployment template. Each is `pending` because the thing to
enumerate is a number in a sentence, or a fact with no field to live in. The design question
is the declaration form; it should be settled with the worked exception idioms (steps 2 and
3) behind it, because a declaration that cannot express its own honest negative will be
written around. The candidates the step 3 exploration surfaced:

* **M17** — a bridge as data on the carrier's instance: `@[carrierBridge "rung" witness]` /
  `@[carrierBridgeFree "reason"]`, checked as `RelationLicense` rungs already are (a named
  theorem, existing, sorry-free). Population unscoped (the worked instance declares no
  carrier instance of its own); `CudaT`/`TapeBuilder` carry `NumCarrier` only; parametric
  instances are derived, not owed a bridge.
* **M20** — two optional `Relation` fields, `wellPosed` (an `∃!`-concluding sorry-free
  theorem) and `domain` (the declared box or predicate), checked in `checkRelation` the way
  `tolerance` is; the sweep is then the `inverts` edges lacking them.
* **M21 (ambiguity)** — a form naming the collision theorem beside the edge, or a port role
  for "ambiguous"; to be chosen with M20's fields, since they share the edge.
* **M22** — either a `Contract.diagnostics` clause (which produced ports are conditioning or
  quality outputs, and what they diagnose) or a `@[kindDiagnostic]` mark on the kind, with
  the census crossing a boundary's kind footprint against its declared ports. The second
  reuses the most machinery and would flag the worked instance's `condPivot` mechanically.

### Step 5 — M7's proxy

Decide whether "every pair of dedicated kinds that meet in one operation signature" is the
claim, or something narrower. Write the decision into the rubric's `closure` string as a
proxy, not as the obligation.

## 4. The rule for calling a step done

A sweep exists when three things are true, in this order:

1. its test probe pins **every verdict class**, including the exempted one, so the
   classifier is exercised rather than the happy path;
2. it has been run against soil-moisture-model's kinds — the only worked instance — and
   the result read, because a sweep that has never met a real vocabulary is a hypothesis
   about one;
3. the rubric's `closure` in the catalogue names it, and `rubric_closure_count` has moved.

Step 3 is the one that changes the published number; steps 1 and 2 are what make the
number true.
