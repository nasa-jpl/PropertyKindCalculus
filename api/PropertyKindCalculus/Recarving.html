    Re-carving a whole #

    source
    structure PropertyKindCalculus.Recarving {O : Type u} (m : Measurement O) :
    Type u

    A re-carving of a whole: a map on carvings, with the obligation that the measured whole is left where it was. The obligation is a field rather than a side condition for the reason WeightedCarving's nonzero total is a field — a map that does not preserve the whole is not a re-carving of it, and should not be a term of this type.

    • map : Decomposition O → Decomposition O

      The map from one carving of the whole to another.

    • preserves (d : Decomposition O) : (m (self.map d)).numeral = (m d).numeral

      The obligation: the whole is the same whole.

      theorem PropertyKindCalculus.Recarving.leafSum_invariant {O : Type u} {k : KindOfProperty} {m : Measurement O} (h : Extensive k m) (r : Recarving m) (d : Decomposition O) :
      leafSum m (r.map d) = leafSum m d

      Every licensed aggregate is re-carving invariant. For an extensive kind the total over the parts is the value of the whole, so a map that preserves the whole preserves the total — whatever it does to the parts. This is the law reduce_sum_eq_sum_toCharges establishes for one functional in one corner of PhysLib, proved once for every extensive kind.

      theorem PropertyKindCalculus.Recarving.distribution_license {O : Type u} (outs : List (KindOfProperty × Measurement O)) (map : Decomposition O → Decomposition O) (hext : ∀ (o : KindOfProperty × Measurement O), o ∈ outs → Extensive o.fst o.snd) (hpres : ∀ (o : KindOfProperty × Measurement O), o ∈ outs → ∀ (d : Decomposition O), (o.snd (map d)).numeral = (o.snd d).numeral) (d : Decomposition O) (o : KindOfProperty × Measurement O) :
      o ∈ outs → leafSum o.snd (map d) = leafSum o.snd d

      The distribution license — the boundary-level form. A module whose batched outputs are all extensive commutes with a re-carving of its batch axis: one map, every output's total preserved, however the axis is cut into tiles, blocks, or shards. Stated over the list of output measurements because a boundary is a list — the quantifier over it is the module-level claim, and each output alone is leafSum_invariant. This is what a declared AggregationClass.extensive clause buys; a count-keyed output is exactly the one it does not cover — coalesce_count_ne exhibits a map that preserves every extensive total while changing the count, which is why a count travels with its carving (per pixel, per shard) rather than with the whole. The §13.5.2 relaxation, where each total stands within joins · t of its whole, is priced in Uncertainty.QuasiExtensive.

        Witness — the whole survives a re-carving, the count does not #

        The simplest whole there is: a part is its mass in whole kilograms, so a carving is a tree of numbers and the whole is their sum. coalesce replaces any carving by the single part whose mass is that total — fewer entities, same whole, the reduce pattern with nothing else in it. Its preserves field is rfl, because the total of a one-leaf carving of the total is the total.

                theorem PropertyKindCalculus.coalesce_count_ne :
                Decomposition.count (fun (x : Int) => true) (coalesce.map twoParts) ≠ Decomposition.count (fun (x : Int) => true) twoParts

                The count does not. The same re-carving takes two parts to one. So there is no function from the whole to how many parts it has, and a library that reports a count is reporting a fact about its carving — which is why a count needs a sortal to be a quantity of the whole at all.

                source
                theorem PropertyKindCalculus.count_sortal_ne :
                Decomposition.count (fun (n : Int) => n == 1) twoParts ≠ Decomposition.count (fun (n : Int) => n == 2) twoParts

                And the sortal is what a count is keyed to. One carving, two predicates, two counts: counting the one-kilogram parts of twoParts gives 2 and counting its two-kilogram parts gives 0. The elementary entity is not recoverable from the whole, which is the content of the SI's insistence that amount of substance is a count of a specified entity.
